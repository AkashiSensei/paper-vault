# Cache-to-Cache: Direct Semantic Communication Between Large Language Models

## Core Contribution

The paper introduces Cache-to-Cache (C2C), a paradigm in which one LLM communicates with another by having a trained neural fuser project and merge its KV-Cache directly into the receiver model's KV-Cache, instead of exchanging natural-language text, yielding higher accuracy and lower latency than text-to-text (T2T) communication.

## Summary

The motivating question is direct: **can LLMs communicate beyond text?** Existing multi-LLM systems make models talk through generated tokens, which forces high-dimensional internal state to be compressed into a low-bandwidth, ambiguous string and then re-encoded by the reader, and which pays token-by-token decoding latency for every exchange.

The paper's throughline proceeds from evidence to mechanism to system:

1. **Oracle motivation.** Two oracle experiments establish that (a) enriching a model's KV-Cache semantics at a *fixed* sequence length already improves accuracy (a cache-enrichment oracle isolates enrichment from added context tokens), and (b) one model's KV-Cache is convertible into another's representation space (a trained MLP maps a source cache into the target's space; layer-wise effects are non-uniform, so only some layers should be enriched).
2. **Paradigm.** Building on those findings, C2C designates a **Sharer** (provides understanding) and a **Receiver** (uses it). A **Cache Fuser** projects and fuses the Sharer's KV-Cache into the Receiver's per-layer KV-Cache with a residual connection, and a **learnable per-layer gate** decides which Receiver layers actually receive injected context.
3. **Cross-model alignment.** Because Sharer and Receiver can differ in family, size, layer count, and tokenizer, C2C aligns them at the token level (re-tokenization with maximal-coverage selection) and layer level (terminal alignment from the output side inward).
4. **Training.** Both models stay frozen; only the C2C fuser is trained, with an SFT-style next-token loss on the Receiver's response conditioned on the fused cache.
5. **Evidence.** Across Qwen2.5/Qwen3/Llama3.2/Gemma3 pairs and four benchmarks, C2C improves the Receiver by roughly 8.5-14% over the model alone and about 3-5% over T2T, while delivering an average ~2.5x latency reduction by skipping the Sharer's intermediate text decode.

The result reframes inter-model communication as an in-representation-space fusion problem rather than a text-messaging problem, and positions KV-Cache as a richer, parallel communication medium.

## Problem

Multi-LLM systems combine complementary models (general, code, math, edge, differently sized) to solve tasks a single model cannot, but in current designs the models communicate only through **text**. Text is a low-bandwidth, lossy, and ambiguous channel: rich internal representations must be serialized to tokens and re-parsed by the receiver, so signals that depend on a model's specific interpretation of a context (for example, treating `<p>` as a paragraph separator rather than an unknown word) can be irrecoverable. Text handoff is also slow, because every exchange requires exhaustive token-by-token decoding of an explanatory message before the receiver can act.

The paper asks whether a richer, faster medium exists. It argues that the KV-Cache—the per-token key/value state a Transformer already computes during prefill—is a naturally richer representation than text and can be transferred by direct projection in parallel, avoiding sequential decoding. The problem is therefore twofold: show that cache-level semantic transfer is *beneficial* and *feasible* across heterogeneous models, and design a mechanism that realizes it without retraining the underlying models.

- Area(s): LLM and Agents.
- Scenario(s): Multi-LLM collaboration and communication, where a Sharer model contributes contextual understanding to a Receiver model that produces the response; heterogeneous model pairing across families, sizes, specializations, and training stages.
- Why it matters here: The contribution is about how LLMs are organized to collaborate and how their internal representations can be reused across models, which is a question about model behavior and use rather than about a serving engine, scheduler, or cluster runtime. It reframes the communication interface of multi-LLM/multi-agent systems.
- Indexing rationale: It belongs in LLM because the object of study is inter-model semantic transfer and its effect on task accuracy; it belongs in Agents because the paradigm targets the communication primitive of collaborative multi-LLM systems and is compared against text-based multi-agent handoff, while the paper itself frames general agentic use as future work rather than a demonstrated deployment.

## Assumptions And Scope

- Stated assumptions:
  - Claim: Sharer and Receiver both expose their per-layer key/value caches so a fuser can read and rewrite them.
    - Key constraint: **white-box access to internal KV-Cache**, not black-box text APIs.
    - Why it matters: Communication happens by projecting and adding to KV tensors in representation space; a model reachable only through a text/completion API cannot participate.
    - Excludes or weakens: Closed commercial endpoints, tool/agent protocols that only exchange text, and any setting where the two models cannot both surface and accept raw KV state.
  - Claim: Communication is trained and evaluated as a fixed **pairwise** Sharer-Receiver relationship.
    - Key constraint: **per-pair trained fuser**.
    - Why it matters: A separate C2C module is trained for each Sharer-Receiver pair; scaling to many models has O(N) or O(N^2) training cost, which the paper treats as an open problem (partial O(N) design in Appendix A.5).
    - Excludes or weakens: Plug-and-play interoperability among arbitrary, previously unseen models without additional training.
  - Claim: The models can be aligned at token and layer granularity for the target inputs.
    - Key constraint: **token re-encoding and terminal layer mapping** across different tokenizers and depths.
    - Why it matters: Fusion adds the Sharer's layer cache to a mapped Receiver layer, so mismatched tokenization or depth must be reconciled or the injected semantics are misaligned.
    - Excludes or weakens: Inputs where tokenizer mismatch cannot be resolved cleanly, or architectures whose layer semantics do not correspond under terminal alignment.
- Implied assumptions:
  - Claim: Sharer and Receiver run in the **same execution context** so KV tensors can be projected and fused in memory during prefill.
    - Key constraint: **co-located in-memory KV access**.
    - Why it matters: The claimed latency advantage comes from replacing the Sharer's text decode with a single parallel cache fusion (~90 ms in the reported breakdown); crossing a process/service boundary would reintroduce KV export, alignment, and copy costs.
    - Excludes or weakens: Two independent inference services communicating over RPC, or agents merely routed to a shared endpoint by a load balancer.
  - Claim: The Sharer's contextual understanding is at least as reliable as the Receiver's on the target inputs.
    - Key constraint: **Sharer semantic quality**.
    - Why it matters: The fused cache can mislead the Receiver when the Sharer is much weaker or wrong; the paper reports failure cases where a weak Sharer degrades a stronger Receiver.
    - Excludes or weakens: Pairings with a noisy or much weaker Sharer relative to the Receiver.
- What is ignored or abstracted away:
  - Claim: Serving-system concerns such as batching, memory paging, multi-tenant scheduling, and cross-request cache reuse are out of scope.
    - Key constraint: **single-pair, batch-size-one evaluation** on one A100.
    - Why it matters: The paper measures the communication paradigm, not throughput under production serving; it is not a serving or KV-paging system.
    - Excludes or weakens: Claims about system throughput, concurrency, or deployment at fleet scale.
- Applies when:
  - Claim: Two (or few) co-located white-box models of complementary strength collaborate on a task, and per-pair fuser training is affordable.
    - Key constraint: **co-located white-box models with a trained fuser**.
    - Why it matters: This is exactly the setting where in-memory cache fusion is both feasible and faster than text handoff.
- May not apply when:
  - Claim: Models are behind text-only APIs, run as separate services, are numerous and unseen, or include a much weaker Sharer.
    - Key constraint: **black-box, distributed, open-set, or low-quality-Sharer settings**.
    - Why it matters: These break the white-box, co-located, per-pair, quality assumptions that make C2C beneficial.

## Core Idea

The key reframing is that **text is not the only interface between LLMs**: the KV-Cache a model already computes is a higher-bandwidth, non-sequential carrier of its contextual understanding, so communication can be a representation-space operation instead of a generate-then-parse loop. Two oracle observations make this credible. First, enriching the KV-Cache of a fixed-length question with information from exemplars improves accuracy without adding context tokens, showing that *how* a context is embedded in the cache—not just how many tokens it spans—carries usable semantic value. Second, a source model's KV-Cache can be projected into a target model's representation space, showing the caches of different models are mutually convertible even though their raw representations occupy different regions.

The mechanism that realizes this idea is a **residual, gated fusion**: rather than overwriting the Receiver's cache (which discards its own understanding), the fuser adds a projected-and-fused contribution to the Receiver's per-layer cache, and a learnable per-layer gate turns injection on only for layers that benefit. This respects the paper's finding that cache enrichment helps some layers and hurts others, and it keeps the Receiver's own competence intact while importing complementary semantics from the Sharer.

## Method

At a high level, C2C augments the Receiver's normal prefill. Both models prefill the same input and produce their own per-token, per-layer KV-Caches. For each Receiver layer `n`, a Cache Fuser `F_n` takes the Receiver's layer-`n` cache and the aligned Sharer layer `G(n)` cache and produces a fused cache that is added back to the Receiver's cache through a residual connection; a learnable gate decides whether layer `n` receives the injection. The Receiver then decodes its response using the fused cache instead of its own, so no intermediate Sharer text is ever generated. Cross-model use requires token- and layer-alignment, and only the fuser is trained while both LLMs stay frozen.

The design rests on the two oracle studies (Section 3.2), then the C2C scheme (Section 3.3): fuser structure (3.3.2), model alignment (3.3.3), and training (3.3.4).

- Component: Cache-enrichment oracle
  - Role: Test whether richer cache semantics help without longer context.
  - Input: A question `X`; exemplars `E`.
  - Output: Accuracy under Direct (`C(X)`), Few-shot (`C(E⊕X)`, longer), and Oracle (prefill `E⊕X` but keep only the question-aligned slice `C*(X)`, same length as Direct).
  - Transformation: Isolates enrichment from added tokens by discarding the exemplar cache before decoding.
  - Minimal example: On MMLU-Redux, Direct = 58.42%, Oracle = 62.34% at the same cache length (Table 1), and single-layer enrichment helps some layers (e.g., layer 4/16) but hurts others (Table 10).
- Component: Cache-transformation oracle
  - Role: Test whether one model's cache is usable by another.
  - Input: Source (Qwen3-4B) last-layer KV-Cache; target (Qwen3-0.6B) cache.
  - Output: A 3-layer MLP mapping source cache into target space (trained with MSE).
  - Transformation: t-SNE shows the transformed source cache lands inside the target's representation space, but covers only a subset—evidence of convertibility plus complementary, non-identical knowledge (Figure 3).
- Component: Cache Fuser (projection + feature fusion + dynamic weighting + gate)
  - Role: Merge Sharer semantics into the Receiver's cache without destructive overwrite.
  - Input: Receiver layer cache `C_n(X)` and aligned Sharer layer cache `C^S_{G(n)}(X)`.
  - Output: Fused layer cache `C_n(X) + F_n(C_n(X), C^S_{G(n)}(X))` when the layer's gate is open.
  - Transformation: (1) Projection concatenates Receiver and Sharer caches and passes them through a projection then a feature-fusion layer; (2) an input-aware head-modulation layer dynamically reweights the fused information per query; (3) a per-layer learnable gate (Gumbel-sigmoid with temperature annealing, differentiable in training and binary at inference) selects which layers are injected.
  - Minimal example: A complex variant (C2C-C) that first projects the Sharer cache to the Receiver's dimensionality via a 3-layer MLP recovers more of the weak-to-strong gap (Table 9), but the paper keeps the simple fuser as default.
- Component: Model alignment (token and layer)
  - Role: Make heterogeneous Sharer/Receiver caches correspond position-by-position and layer-by-layer.
  - Input: Two tokenizations and two layer stacks (possibly different depths).
  - Output: A per-token Sharer-to-Receiver mapping and a Receiver-layer-to-Sharer-layer mapping `G`.
  - Transformation: Token alignment decodes each Receiver token to text and re-encodes with the Sharer tokenizer, using maximal-coverage selection for one-to-many cases and padding template sections; layer alignment uses terminal alignment (final layers paired first, then inward) rather than depth-normalized alignment.
  - Minimal example: With a deeper Sharer, its last layer maps to the Receiver's last layer, penultimate to penultimate, and so on until the shallower model's first layer.
- Component: Training scheme
  - Role: Learn only the fuser while preserving both models.
  - Input: Paired inputs (default OpenHermes-2.5, 500k samples) and the two frozen models.
  - Output: A trained per-pair C2C module.
  - Transformation: Three stages—Forward (both models prefill to produce caches), Fusion (C2C fuses and replaces the Receiver's cache), Supervision (Receiver prefills the response on the fused cache and next-token/SFT loss backpropagates through C2C only).
  - Minimal example: With Qwen2.5-0.5B Sharer + Qwen3-0.6B Receiver, ~300 steps (<9 GPU hours) already approach the final checkpoint's accuracy (Table 12).

## Experiments And Evidence

- **C2C vs. individual models → cache fusion adds real accuracy over the Receiver alone.**
  - Selected evidence: Averaged over four benchmarks, C2C raises Receiver (Qwen3-0.6B) accuracy by 11.00%, 9.64%, and 11.88% for the three Sharers; the abstract summarizes 6.4-14.2% over individual models.
  - Supports: The Receiver genuinely benefits from the Sharer's contextual understanding, not just from extra trainable parameters.
  - Original: Section 4.2 and Table 4; abstract.
- **C2C vs. Text-to-Text (T2T) → richer channel beats text handoff on both accuracy and latency.**
  - Selected evidence: C2C exceeds T2T by 5.36%, 4.15%, and 3.06% average accuracy across the three Sharers, and by 8.59% average across five different model combinations (Table 7); latency speedups over T2T are 3.46x, 1.51x, and 14.41x (average ~2.5x). The breakdown shows T2T's Sharer decodes 80 tokens (1312 ms) while C2C replaces this with 90 ms parallel fusion (Table 3).
  - Supports: Skipping intermediate text decoding is the main latency win, and cache-level transfer conveys semantics text loses.
  - Original: Section 4.2, Tables 3-4, Section 4.3, Table 7.
- **C2C vs. query-level routing → routing caps at the better single model.**
  - Selected evidence: Query-level routing prioritizes efficiency but limits accuracy to the stronger of the two models and underperforms C2C.
  - Supports: Selecting one model per query cannot combine complementary understanding the way fusion can.
  - Original: Section 4.2 and Table 4.
- **Ablation: source of improvement → gains come from heterogeneous complementarity, not capacity.**
  - Selected evidence: Single (fine-tune Receiver, no Sharer) < Identical (C2C with same model as both) < C2C (different models); e.g., on MMLU 36.81 / 42.17 / 42.92, with C2C using fewer parameters than Identical (Table 6).
  - Supports: Improvements are not merely added trainable capacity or overfitting; heterogeneous Sharer context is the driver, though same-model self-communication still helps somewhat.
  - Original: Section 4.4 and Table 6.
- **Ablation: fuser components → fuse-with-residual and gating both matter.**
  - Selected evidence: Pure projection (discarding Receiver cache) averages 20.70%; fusing both caches via residual raises it by 24.18% to 44.88%; adding the per-layer gate adds 3.07% to 47.95% (Table 8).
  - Supports: Retaining the Receiver's own cache (residual) is essential, and selective per-layer injection helps.
  - Original: Section 4.4 and Table 8.
- **Behavior: effective rank → fusion enriches representation.**
  - Selected evidence: After C2C, average effective rank rises from 388 to 395 (K) and 532 to 560 (V) relative to the Receiver (Table 2).
  - Supports: C2C increases the intrinsic dimensionality/semantic richness of the Receiver's cache.
  - Original: Section 4.5 and Table 2 (definition in Appendix A.4.1).
- **Scaling → gains hold across sequence length and model size.**
  - Selected evidence: On LongBenchV1, C2C beats T2T across 0-4k, 4-8k, and 8k+ intervals (Table 5); accuracy gains of C2C over the Receiver generally grow faster than T2T as the Sharer gets larger (Figure 6). Swapping Sharer/Receiver, C2C still adds 5.05% while T2T drops 6.30%.
  - Supports: The paradigm is robust across input lengths and Sharer capacities, and is less brittle than text handoff under role swaps.
  - Original: Section 4.3, Tables 5 and 7, Figure 6.
- **Negative and qualitative evidence.**
  - Selected evidence: A weak Sharer can transmit wrong understanding and pull a stronger Receiver to the wrong answer (accounting example, Appendix A.4.6); Qwen3-4B-Base as Sharer follows instructions poorly alone yet still helps via C2C. A preliminary GSM8K agentic-flow case (Appendix A.5.3) reports Single 41.17, T2T 61.18, C2C 62.55, and T-C2C 78.01.
  - Supports: Benefits depend on Sharer quality; C2C can complement (not just replace) text in agentic flows, but multi-model scaling remains preliminary.
  - Original: Sections 4.5, 5, and Appendices A.4.6, A.5.
  - Boundary note: All efficiency numbers are single-A100, batch-size-one measurements; several diagnostics fix Qwen3 Sharer/Receiver to remove alignment confounds, and some analyses use the stronger C2C-C variant.

## Contributions

- Poses and answers the question of whether LLMs can communicate beyond text, with oracle experiments showing KV-Cache enrichment helps at fixed length and that caches are convertible across models.
- Introduces the Cache-to-Cache (C2C) paradigm: direct projection and fusion of a Sharer's KV-Cache into a Receiver's cache via a trained neural fuser with residual integration.
- Designs a fuser with projection/feature-fusion, input-aware dynamic weighting, and a learnable per-layer gate, plus token- and layer-alignment for heterogeneous model pairs, trained with an SFT-style loss while both LLMs stay frozen.
- Demonstrates consistent accuracy gains over individual models, text-to-text communication, and query routing, with an average ~2.5x latency reduction, across multiple families, sizes, specializations, and benchmarks.

## Limitations

- Performance degrades when a much weaker Sharer feeds noisy information to a stronger Receiver; Sharer semantic quality directly bounds Receiver quality (a limitation shared with T2T).
- Communication is trained per Sharer-Receiver pair; scaling to many models has O(N)/O(N^2) training cost and remains an open problem (only preliminary multi-Sharer/Receiver designs in Appendix A.5).
- The method requires white-box, co-located models that expose and accept raw KV-Cache; it does not apply to text-only APIs or independent networked services.
- Evaluation is on multiple-choice/knowledge/long-context benchmarks with small-to-mid models (0.6B-14B), batch size one on a single A100; production serving throughput, concurrency, and much larger models are not measured.
- Some analyses rely on the more powerful C2C-C variant or fix Qwen3 pairs, so certain diagnostic numbers are not the default simple fuser under fully heterogeneous pairing.

## Key Takeaways

- KV-Cache can serve as a richer, parallel communication medium between LLMs, replacing lossy, sequential text handoff.
- Cache enrichment at fixed length improves accuracy, and cross-model caches are convertible—these two oracle facts underpin the whole paradigm.
- Residual, gated fusion (not overwrite) is what preserves the Receiver's competence while importing the Sharer's complementary semantics.
- The main latency win comes from eliminating the Sharer's intermediate text decode, not from cheaper attention.
- C2C is a trained, per-pair capability with white-box, co-located assumptions—closer to a fixed model-pair accelerator than a universal agent protocol.

These points are discussion-level interpretation, separated from the paper-grounded sections above.

1. 部署假设：C2C（对应论文与代码中的 `RosettaModel`）假设多个模型共处于同一进程/运行时中，因此可以在内存里直接对 KV 张量做投影与融合。它不是“多个 agent 经由负载均衡路由到同一个 vLLM 端点”，也不是“同一台机器上两个相互独立、通过 RPC 通信的推理服务”。如果按后者去实现，就需要 KV 导出、序列对齐以及跨进程拷贝，而这些开销足以抵消论文所声称的延迟优势（论文中约 90ms 的并行缓存融合正是建立在同进程内存访问之上，见 Table 3）。
2. “一个引擎”不等于“一个模型”：讨论中澄清了一个常见混淆——同一个多模型推理引擎可以同时加载 Receiver 与 Sharer，二者的层数、注意力头数可以不同；正是这种不匹配，才需要 Neural Fuser（以及论文的 token/层对齐）来把两个模型的表示对上。
3. 与同模型前缀缓存不同：C2C 区别于 PagedAttention / DeepSeek 式的自动前缀缓存（同一个模型、精确前缀复用、按 LRU/TTL 老化页面）。C2C 做的是跨模型的 KV“翻译/融合”，而不是同模型缓存的复用。聊天机器人多轮对话常常是把历史重新 POST 一遍，其“命中”约等于短暂的前缀匹配，而不是为每个用户长期保留的一格 KV“抽屉”。
4. Fuser 的训练（讨论小结）：冻结 Sharer 与 Receiver，只训练 C2C 模块；在 Receiver 上用下一 token 预测损失、并以融合后的 KV 为条件（类似 SFT）。带有可学习的按层门控（gate）。这意味着：互操作性变成了“针对每一对模型”的训练问题，而不是一个即插即用的通用协议。
5. 实验相对 T2T：与文本交接相比（Sharer 把分析文本写出来、拼接给 Receiver）、以及与单模型、查询路由相比，论文报告的收益大致是——相对单模型有更高准确率、相对 T2T 高几个百分点，同时因为省掉了中间的文本解码而带来约 2× 量级的延迟收益（需对照 PDF 核对具体数字与出处：相对单模型平均约 6.4-14.2%、相对 T2T 约 3.1-5.4%、平均约 2.5× 加速，见 Abstract 与 Section 4.2、Table 3-4）。
6. 可用性结论：更应把 C2C 看作“共处一地的、固定模型对的加速器”，而不是任意 agent 之间的通用 Agent 中间件 / “USB”。文本仍然是那条通用但有损的总线；C2C 在满足白盒、同进程、成对训练等前提时才成立。

### English Translation

1. Deployment assumption: C2C (the `RosettaModel` in the paper/code) assumes multiple models are co-located in one process/runtime, so KV tensors can be projected and fused in memory. It is NOT "many agents routed by a load balancer into the same vLLM endpoint", and NOT "two independent inference services on one machine talking over RPC". Implementing the latter would require KV export, sequence alignment, and cross-process copies whose overhead can erase the claimed latency win—the paper's ~90 ms parallel cache fusion (Table 3) presupposes in-process memory access.
2. "One engine" is not "one model": the discussion clarified a common confusion—one multi-model engine can load both Receiver and Sharer with different layer counts and head counts; that mismatch is exactly why a Neural Fuser (and the paper's token/layer alignment) exists.
3. Not the same as same-model prefix caching: C2C is distinct from PagedAttention / DeepSeek-style automatic prefix caching (same model, exact prefix reuse, LRU/TTL page aging). C2C performs cross-model KV "translation/fusion", not same-model cache reuse. Chatbot multi-turn traffic often re-POSTs the whole history, so its "cache hit" is roughly a short-lived prefix match, not a permanent per-user KV drawer.
4. Fuser training (discussion summary): freeze Sharer and Receiver, and train only the C2C module, using next-token loss on the Receiver conditioned on the fused KV-Cache (SFT-like), with a learnable per-layer gate. Implication: interoperability becomes a per model-pair training problem, not a plug-and-play protocol.
5. Experiments vs. T2T: compared to text handoff (the Sharer writes an analysis text that is concatenated for the Receiver), to individual models, and to query routing, the reported gains are roughly higher accuracy than single models and a few points over T2T, with an ~2x-scale latency benefit from skipping the intermediate decode (verify exact numbers and locations against the PDF: ~6.4-14.2% over individual models, ~3.1-5.4% over T2T, and ~2.5x average speedup, per the Abstract and Section 4.2, Tables 3-4).
6. Usability takeaway: C2C is better viewed as a co-located, fixed model-pair accelerator than as general Agent middleware / a "USB" between arbitrary agents. Text remains the universal but lossy bus; C2C holds only under white-box, in-process, per-pair-trained conditions.

  - Status: not-ingested
